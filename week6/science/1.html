Forces in Action

Pushes, pulls, and what happens when they do not cancel out. Five rounds, at your own pace.

Five rounds on forces

Name the forces, add them up, sort out springs, work out what makes things speed up or stop, and tell friction from drag. Do them in order or skip about. Nothing here is timed.

The big idea. A force is a push or a pull. When two forces on an object are equal and opposite, they are balanced, and nothing about its motion changes. When they are unbalanced, the object speeds up, slows down, or changes direction. Force is measured in newtons (N).

Round 1: Spot the force

Every push or pull has a name. Match each description to the right force.

Round 2: Add up the forces

Same direction, add them. Opposite directions, subtract them. The answer is the overall force, sometimes called the resultant. Type the number, pick the unit direction, then say if it is balanced.

Round 3: Springs and stretching

When you pull a spring, you do work on it, and that energy has to go somewhere.

Round 4: Changing motion

It takes an unbalanced force to start something, stop it, or turn it. Two quick answers, then the big one.

A football rolls across the grass and slowly stops. Name the force rubbing against it that slows it down.
A player runs up and kicks the rolling ball from the side. What happens to the ball's direction of motion?

A spaceship coasts through deep, empty space. No air resistance, no friction. It switches its engines fully off.

Will it slow down and stop? Explain using what you know about forces.
No, it will not slow down. With no friction and no air resistance, there is no unbalanced force acting on it, so it keeps moving at the same speed in the same direction.

Round 5: Friction or drag?

Friction is solid surfaces rubbing together. Drag is pushing through a gas or a liquid, like air resistance or water resistance. Sort each one.

That is the lot

The thread through all five rounds: nothing changes its motion on its own. It always takes an unbalanced force. If you remember only that, you are most of the way there.
