Evidence and Bias

How do we know what really happened? Today is detective work with sources, artefacts and quiet one-sided clues.

Well done.

Round 1: Source words

Historians do not just ask, "What does this say?" They ask who made it, when it was made, and whether it might be leaning one way.

Key idea: evidence is useful, but it still needs checking. A source can be true, false, useful, biased, or a mixture of all of those annoying things.

Round 2: Fact or opinion?

A fact can be checked. An opinion is what someone thinks or feels. Both can appear in historical sources.

Round 3: The suspicious factory owner

Read the source. It was written during the Industrial Revolution by a factory owner in 1832.

"My factory is a wonderful place for children to work. The air is warm and clean. The children are happy and sing songs while they spin cotton. They are lucky I give them a job."- Written by Mr. Thomas Grind, Factory Owner, 1832
Fast checks
Bias thinking
Sentence frame: This source may be biased because the writer is a _____ and wants people to think _____.

Round 4: Artefact detective

Objects from the past can be sources too. This medieval coin shows a king sending a message about himself.

Round 5: Two versions of one event

Historians compare sources because different people can tell the same event in completely different ways.

Source A

"The peasants were wicked rebels. They acted like wild dogs, destroying everything and murdering good, rich men."

Written by an English monk who owned a lot of land, 1381.

Source B

"The poor workers were starving and angry. The unfair taxes forced them to march to London to ask the King for help."

Written by a modern historian, 2015.

Finish: What would a historian do?

A useful historian does not just believe the loudest source. They compare, question and explain.

A new source says: "The king was perfect and everyone loved him." It was written by someone who worked for the king. How useful is this source? What might it tell us, and what might it hide?

Useful structure: This source is useful because... However, it might be biased because... I would compare it with...
