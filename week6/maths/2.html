Data and Averages

Finding the middle of a muddle: mean, median and mode, and when each one is the honest choice.

Well done!

Six quick rounds

Build the vocabulary, find averages from a slightly suspicious gaming tournament, compare a mean with and without an outlier, read charts, and send your work to Jay. Do it in order or skip about. Nothing here is timed.

Four average words. Mean is the shared-out total. Median is the middle value. Mode is the most common value. Range is the spread from smallest to biggest.
Big idea: an average can be correct but still not tell the full story. The honest choice depends on what the data looks like.

Round 1: Average memory match

Match each data word to its meaning. The aim is not speed; the aim is choosing the right maths tool at the right time.

Useful clue: an outlier is a value that is much bigger or much smaller than most of the others.

Averages are not just about calculating. They are about choosing a number that tells the fairest story.

A claim says: "The mean is always the best average because it uses all the numbers." Analyse the claim. When is the mean useful? When might the median or mode tell a fairer story?
