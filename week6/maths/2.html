Understanding Data and Averages

This activity is about finding useful numbers from a set of data. It covers mean, median, mode, range, outliers, bar charts, pie charts and pictograms. It usually takes about 35 to 45 minutes. You can jump around, stop early, or come back later. Jay can check whatever you choose to send.

Step 1 of 7

Set up the data lab

Averages help turn a list of numbers into one number that gives a quick idea of the group. Different averages tell different stories.

The mean can be useful, but an outlier can pull it around like a tiny maths tractor. Suspiciously powerful for one number.

Today you could practise

1. Matching average words to meanings.

2. Finding mode, median, range and mean.

3. Spotting an outlier.

4. Reading bar charts, pie charts and pictograms.

Deep link for older learners: there is not always one "best" average. The best choice depends on the question and the shape of the data.
Step 2 of 7 · Predict first

Average memory match

Before matching the words, predict one: what might an outlier be in a set of data?

Clue: an outlier is a value that is much bigger or much smaller than most of the others.

Match each word to its meaning

Deep link for older learners: the mean uses every value, so it is sensitive to outliers. The median only cares about the middle position.
Step 3 of 7

Gaming tournament data

Seven friends played a video game. Their scores are already in order.

5 5 5 7 8 10 44
Deep link for older learners: after removing 44, the new mean is about 6.6. That better represents most of the scores because most players scored between 5 and 10.
Step 4 of 7

Mean machine: with and without the outlier

Tap the button to compare the mean with 44 included and with 44 removed. This is a quick way to see how one unusual number can change the story.

Without 44

5, 5, 5, 7, 8, 10

Deep link for older learners: a statistic can be accurate and still slightly misleading if it hides the shape of the data.
