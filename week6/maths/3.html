Place Value

What each digit is really worth, and why one small zero can change everything.

Star earned!

Six short rounds

You will work with place value, negative numbers, decimals, fractions, real-life problems and visual matching. Nothing is timed. You can skip forward, come back, or send whatever you complete.

Big idea: a digit is not just a digit. The place it sits in tells you its value. In 8,204.37, the 2 is worth 200, but the 7 is worth 0.07.

Round 1: Number zoom

Look closely at 8,204.37. The same digit can mean very different things depending on where it sits.

Useful check: multiplying by 10 moves every digit one place bigger. Dividing by 100 moves every digit two places smaller.

Round 2: Order the numbers

Tap the numbers in order from smallest to largest. Negative numbers are smaller than positive numbers. The more negative a number is, the smaller it is.

Your order

Thinking tip: compare negative numbers first, then positive decimals. 3.05 is smaller than 3.1 because 3.1 is the same as 3.10.

Round 3: Core calculations

A mixture of negatives and decimals. Use the reminder if the signs start being dramatic.

Negative reminder: adding a negative is like subtracting. Subtracting a negative is like adding. Two negatives multiplied together make a positive.

Round 4: Fraction focus

Fractions are not separate from place value. They are another way of showing parts of a whole.

Open a fraction hint

For addition and subtraction, make the denominators match. For multiplication, multiply the top numbers and multiply the bottom numbers.

Round 5: Real-life problems

These are still calculation questions, but now the units matter.

Route: start below zero, then add the money paid in.

Unit clue: 85 cm is 0.85 m. Convert first, then subtract.
