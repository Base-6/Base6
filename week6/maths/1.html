Area and Volume

How much space does something cover, and how much can it hold? Two different questions with two different answers.

Well done!

Six calm rounds

Start with squares, sort the formula shortcuts, solve some shape questions, move into volume, tackle real problems, then send your work to Jay. Do it in order or skip about. Nothing here is timed.

Two words for today. Area measures flat space, so the units are squared, like cm² or m². Volume measures space inside a 3D shape, so the units are cubed, like cm³ or m³.
Quick idea: a formula is not magic. It is a shortcut for a shape pattern. The harder bit is choosing the right shortcut.

Round 1: Count the space

This rectangle is 4 squares wide and 3 squares tall. Before using the formula, count or predict the number of squares it covers.

4 columns and 3 rows

Rectangle rule: area = width × height. Here that means 4 × 3 = 12 squares.
