Base 6 · Monday Maths

Area and Volume Lab

This is a calm maths activity about space inside shapes. It usually takes about 30 to 40 minutes. You can jump around, stop early, come back later, or just try the bits that feel useful. Jay will be able to see what you send at the end.

Step 1 of 7

Set up the lab

Area is the amount of flat space covered. Volume is the amount of 3D space inside something. This lab uses rectangles, triangles, trapezia and prisms.

Nothing here locks you in. If a question feels annoying, you can leave it and still send Jay what you did.

Today you could practise

1. Counting squares and using width x height.

2. Spotting when a formula includes a half.

3. Using front face area x length for volume.

4. Choosing sensible units: cm², m², cm³ or m³.

Deep link for older learners: a formula is just a shortcut for a repeated structure. The trick is spotting which structure you are looking at.
Step 2 of 7 · Predict first

Rectangle rescue

Before revealing the rule, have a prediction. This rectangle is 4 squares wide and 3 squares tall.

Rectangle rule: area = width x height.

Here that is 4 x 3 = 12 squares.

Deep link for older learners: doubling the width doubles the area, as long as the height stays the same. Area changes fast because two directions matter.
Step 3 of 7

Formula sorter

Some shapes are simple multiply jobs. Some include a half or a divide by 2 because the shape is half of a larger shape. Sort each rule into the best bin.

Deep link for older learners: triangles and trapezia often use a rectangle or parallelogram hiding in the background. The divide by 2 is not random; it corrects a doubled area.
Step 4 of 7

Shape workshop

Pick an answer card. If the first pick is not the one, the hint will show a calmer route through the problem.

Deep link for older learners: a reverse problem works backwards from the area. For a triangle, double the area first, then divide by the height to find the base.
Step 5 of 7

Volume tunnel

Volume is space inside a 3D shape. For a prism, one reliable route is: area of the front face x length.

Deep link for older learners: square units measure flat space. Cubic units measure layers of flat space stacked along a third direction.
Step 6 of 7

Problem solving workshop

These are the more realistic questions. You can try one, a few, or all of them. Units are part of the thinking.

Garden sequence

A trapezium garden has parallel sides 12 m and 18 m, height 10 m, and grass seed costs £2 for each 1 m².

Put the steps in a sensible order.

    Garden numbers

    Pool volume

    A rectangular swimming pool is 25 m long, 10 m wide and 2 m deep.

    Flower bed compost

    A triangular flower bed has base 6 m and height 3 m. One bag of compost covers 3 m².

    Storage container

    The front face area is 12 m². The container is 5 m long. It stores 2 large boxes in each 1 m³.

    Deep link for older learners: word problems are not harder because the maths changes. They are harder because you decide which measurements belong together.

    Reflection and send to Jay

    BOSS LEVEL Optional: for anyone who fancies a proper think

    A claim says: "A triangle with base 12 cm and height 5 cm has area 60 cm2 because 12 x 5 = 60." Analyse the claim. What is useful, what is missing, and what would the area be?
