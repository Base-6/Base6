Base 6 · Monday English

Expressing Yourself Clearly

This activity is about saying what you mean in a clear way. It covers Standard English, staying on topic, explaining feelings, and building a short speech. It usually takes about 30 to 40 minutes. You can jump around, skip bits, stop early, or come back later. Jay can check whatever you choose to send.

Step 1 of 7

Set up the speaking lab

Standard English is formal language. It is useful in school, work, presentations, interviews, emails, and moments where being understood matters.

Informal language is not bad. It just fits different situations. A group chat and a school presentation do not always want the same voice. Annoying, but true.

Today you could practise

1. Swapping informal phrases into Standard English.

2. Spotting an off-topic sentence.

3. Explaining feelings with reasons.

4. Planning a short speech with a clear opinion.

Deep link for older learners: clarity is partly about audience. Strong communicators adjust their style without pretending to be someone else.
Step 2 of 7 · Predict first

Phrase swap

Before the matching game, predict one. What would be a clearer Standard English version of: "I dunno what you mean"?

Clue: Standard English usually avoids slang and shortened informal words. "Dunno" becomes "do not know".

Match each informal phrase to a Standard English version

Deep link for older learners: Standard English is not about being posh. It is about choosing language that works across more audiences.
Step 3 of 7

Off-topic detective

Keeping to the point means staying with the main topic. This mini speech is about school uniforms. One sentence wanders off for a little shopping trip.

Why does that sentence not fit?

Deep link for older learners: a sentence can be grammatically fine and still weaken a speech if it does not support the point.
Step 4 of 7

Feelings and reasons

Clear opinions often include a feeling and a reason. Pick a useful feeling word, then add your own "because" sentence.

Deep link for older learners: feelings become clearer when you link them to evidence. "I felt proud because..." is stronger than just "I felt proud."
Step 5 of 7

Short speech planner

Choose one topic, then build a short speech. The sentence starters are there as scaffolding. You can use them, change them, or ignore them if you already have a better route.

Speech preview

Deep link for older learners: a strong speech usually has a claim, a reason, and a closing line that brings the listener back to the main idea.
