Writing for a Reason

Choosing the right style for the job. A message, a speech, a poster and a story all want different choices.

Saved.

Five quick rounds

Spot the purpose, choose the audience, repair Sam's letter, write for three different reasons, then turn a chatty text into a formal email. Do it in order or skip around. Nothing is timed.

Two words for today. The purpose is why a text was written: to inform, persuade, entertain, instruct or advise. The audience is who will read it. Get both right, and the writing does its job.

Today you could practise

1. Using clue words to work out purpose.

2. Matching writing to the reader.

3. Replacing slang with formal choices.

4. Writing the same event in different ways.

Theme park context for the purpose questions.

Round 1: Spot the purpose

Five theme park sentences. What is the writer trying to do? The clue is in the words they chose.

Round 2: Who is reading?

Audience means the person or group reading your writing. Choose who each text is aimed at.

Round 3: Fix Sam's letter

Sam is writing to the Headteacher, but the language is too chatty. Tap a highlighted phrase, then pick a formal replacement from the word bank.

Dear Headteacher,

I think the new school rules are a bit . We are about having to wear ties. It is a . Please change it back,

,
Sam

Formal and ready to send. Sam has stopped writing like the Headteacher is in a group chat.

Round 4: The dog in the hall

Same event, three different purposes. The event stays the same, but the voice changes.

Purpose: inform parents
Today, a stray dog entered the school because...
Today, a stray dog entered the school because the side gate was left open. It was safely removed and no one was hurt.
Purpose: entertain a friend
OMG! You won't believe it, but a massive dog just...
OMG! You won't believe it, but a massive dog just ran off with the Headteacher's sandwich!
Purpose: instruct students
If you see the dog in the hall, you must...
If you see the dog in the hall, you must stay very quiet and tell a teacher.

Round 5: The rewriting challenge

Turn this chatty message to a friend into a polite, formal email to Mr Jones.

To: Bestie ❤️
yo missed lessons today cuz i was well poorly can u tell me what we did in english also did we get any homework? thx mate!

Email checklist

Tap each one when your email includes it.

Dear Mr Jones, I am writing to explain that I was unable to attend school today because I was unwell. Could you please let me know what work we covered in English? I would also be grateful to know if any homework was set. Thank you for your help. Yours sincerely, [name]

Finish and send

The main question: who is reading this, and what do I want my writing to do?

These questions are less about rules and more about judgement. Choose the answer that fits the audience and purpose best.
