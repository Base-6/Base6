📢 Investigating Propaganda Posters

How wartime posters used tricks to change how people felt — and how to spot the bias.

During the First World War, the government used posters to get men to join the army. These posters used tricks to change how people felt. This is called propaganda.

Poster 1: "Your Country Needs YOU"

The poster: A stern army officer (Lord Kitchener) stares straight out and points his finger at the viewer. Big letters read "YOUR COUNTRY NEEDS YOU".

1. Lord Kitchener is pointing his finger directly at me (the viewer).

2. What feeling is the poster trying to create? Tick two from the word bank.

🧠 Bias means only showing one side of a story to change someone's mind. Propaganda posters are very biased!

3. How does this poster show bias? What does it forget to tell us?

The poster makes joining the army look like a proud and important / dutiful thing to do. However, it does not show the scary parts of war, such as fighting in the mud or being in danger. This means the poster is biased because it only shows the good / positive side.

Poster 2: "Daddy, what did YOU do in the Great War?"

The poster: A father sits in an armchair, looking away and worried. His daughter reads a history book on his knee, and his son plays with toy soldiers on the floor. The words ask "Daddy, what did YOU do in the Great War?"

1. Look at the children in the poster. What are they doing?

The girl is reading a history book and the boy is playing with toy soldiers.

2. How is the father feeling? How do his face and posture show this?

He looks sad and worried, and looks away from the children because he feels guilty he did not fight.

3. Why is this poster a good example of bias?

It makes the reader think that men who did not fight are cowards / ashamed. It forgets to tell us that some men had to stay at home to do important jobs. So it only shows one negative side of not joining up.
