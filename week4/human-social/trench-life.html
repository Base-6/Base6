📋 Trench Life Analysis

Life in the trenches in the First World War — and how we work out what really happened.

1. The Trenches

Let's see what you remember about life in the trenches.

1. When did the First World War happen?

2. Tick the two foods soldiers usually ate in the trenches.

3. What was 'Trench Foot'?

It happened because soldiers stood in cold water and mud for a long time.

4. What was the dangerous land between the two enemy trenches called?

It was called No Man's Land.

5. Why did soldiers build trenches in zig-zag lines?

So that if an enemy got inside, they could not shoot straight down the line. It also helped stop blasts from bombs.

2. Looking at Evidence

Word bank: Primary Source (from the time) · Secondary Source (made later) · Bias (not telling the whole truth) · Artefact (an old object)

1. Something made at the time of the event is a Primary Source.

2. Something made after the event is a Secondary Source.

3. An object from the past (like a helmet) is an Artefact.

4. When a source is not fair and only shows one side, it has Bias.

5. Give one reason a soldier writing home might not say how bad the trenches were.

The soldier might not want to worry his family. He might also be afraid the army would censor / block his letter.

3. Source Analysis

Source A: A photo from a British newspaper in 1916. It shows soldiers resting and smiling.

1. What are the soldiers doing in this photo? They are resting and smiling.

2. Is this a primary or secondary source? It is a primary source, because it was taken at the time.

3. Does this photo show the true horrors of war? No, because the soldiers look clean and happy. It does not show things like fighting or mud / rats.

4. Why did the newspaper choose smiling soldiers? They wanted families to feel good / calm about the war, and to think the soldiers were okay / safe.

4. Comparing Sources

Source B: A diary by Private John Smith, winter 1917.
"The mud here is terrible. It is up to our knees. We are freezing cold and always wet. The rats are as big as cats and they steal our food. We just want to go home, but we have to keep fighting."

1. How deep was the mud? It was up to their knees.

2. What animal problem did they have? There were huge rats that stole their food.

3. Which source shows what life was really like? I think Source B is more real because it talks about mud, rats and being cold. Source A only shows people smiling.

5. Historical Detective

A poster from 1915 says joining the army is a "fun adventure". Is this poster biased (not telling the whole truth)?

It is biased because it says war is fun but we know life in the trenches was actually terrible / dangerous.
