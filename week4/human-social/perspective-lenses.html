👓 The Perspective Lenses

One day on the Western Front, 1916. Five people would tell that day five different ways. You are the historian: study each source, weigh them up, and work out what really happened.

Stage 1 of 5

Meet the five sources

Here is the same day, seen by five people. Tap each one to read what they say, then answer the question underneath. The picture changes to match whose eyes you are looking through.

The same day, before anyone tells the story.

You do not have to write a perfect answer for each — a sentence is fine. Everything saves automatically.

Stage 2 of 5

Primary or secondary?

A primary source was made at the time, by someone who was there. A secondary source was made later, by someone studying it. Sort each one. (Careful — a source can be primary and still be biased!)

Stage 3 of 5

What did the newspaper leave out?

The newspaper photo was a real primary source — but a biased one. Below are five things that truly happened that day. Tick the ones the cheerful newspaper report would have left out.

Stage 4 of 5

The coverage grid

Now compare the sources side by side. Tick which themes each source talks about. When you are done, tap Show how a historian filled it in to compare — and notice how differently each source covers the same day.

Stage 5 of 5

Write your balanced account

You are the historian. Using what you have learned, write a short, fair account of what this day was really like. The checklist shows what a strong account includes — tick each as you add it.

Aim for around 4–6 sentences. Use at least two of the sources, and show that you know one of them is biased.
