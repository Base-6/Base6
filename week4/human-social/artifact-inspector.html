Human and Social · Week 4

🔍 The Artifact Inspector

An object was dug up that no one fully understands. It is carved from bone and fits in one hand. Be the historian: examine it closely, weigh the theories, and reach your own careful verdict.

Stage 1 of 5

Examine the object

Tap each glowing spot to look closely. For each one, read what an expert noticed, then write what you see in your own words. Good historians record carefully before they decide anything.

Spots examined: 0 / 6

Try to examine all six spots before moving on.

Stage 2 of 5

Fact or theory?

An observation is something we can actually see on the object. An interpretation is what someone thinks it means. Historians must not mix them up. Sort each statement.

Stage 3 of 5

Weigh the three theories

Three historians have each studied your object. They used the same clues, yet reached different answers. For each theory, tick the clues you think support it. Then reveal which clues each historian points to.

When you have weighed all three, continue — but hold on, the laboratory has just sent something in...

Stage 4 of 5

New evidence arrives

Laboratory report

The object was not found lying alone. It was buried carefully beside a human skeleton, in a grave, together with a necklace of shells and a polished stone. Tests also show the red colour in the cut lines is ochre — a pigment often used in special ceremonies.

Historians change their minds when new evidence appears. That is good thinking, not weak thinking.

After this report, which theory now seems strongest to you?

Stage 5 of 5

Your verdict

Time to decide. Pick the theory you find strongest, then write your argument. A strong verdict uses the evidence — and is honest that another historian might disagree.
