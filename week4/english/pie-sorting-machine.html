🥧 The PIE Sorting Machine

Every piece of writing has a job. PIE stands for Persuade, Inform, Entertain. Learn to spot the job, then write with a job of your own.

Stage 1 of 5 · Meet PIE

The three jobs of writing

When a writer sits down, they pick a job first. Here are the three jobs you will look for this week.

PersuadeMake you want to do or buy something. (Adverts, posters, "you should…")
InformGive you true facts. (Instructions, signs, the news.)
EntertainMake you smile, laugh or enjoy a story. (Jokes, comics, tales.)

Warm-up: read each one and tap its job.

"You should definitely come to my party — it will be amazing!"
"The library is open from 9am until 4pm."
"My dog ate my homework… and then asked for pudding!"

Stage 4 of 5 · Spot the Spin

One fact, three jobs

Here is a plain fact: "It rained today." A writer can use the same fact for any job — it is the words they choose that change. Match each version to its job, then write the word that gave it away.

"Rainy days are the best — grab your wellies and come and splash in the puddles with me!"

Stage 5 of 5 · Purposeful Composition

Now you choose the job

Pick a goal, then write about school lunches in a way that does that job. The checklist ticks itself as you hit each target — it is a helper, not a test.

  • Choose a goal above
  • Write at least three sentences
  • Use words that match your job
Persuade: "We really should have more choice at lunch. Please could we add a pasta bar? It would be the best change ever and everyone would eat more."
Inform: "Lunch is at 12 o'clock in the hall. You line up, choose a hot meal or a sandwich, then sit with your class."
Entertain: "Today my jelly escaped! It wobbled off my tray, bounced twice, and landed right on Mr Cook's shoe!"
