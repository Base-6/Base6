🤖 The Chatbot Rules Builder

Program a simple keyword chatbot, then test where it breaks. Can it hold a normal conversation, or is it only matching words?

Stage 1 of 5 · Hook

Why do some automated chats feel frustrating?

Early chatbots often worked by looking for keywords. If a sentence contained the word weather, the bot replied with its weather response — even if the person said, “I do not care about the weather.”

User says:
“weather”
→
Bot replies:
“It is sunny!”

Stage 2 of 5 · Build

Program up to 10 rules

A rule has two parts: a keyword to listen for and a response to say. Try to build a bot that can survive exactly three text exchanges before it gives a strange answer.

Rules Builder

Write one keyword in each left box. The bot checks for that word anywhere in the sentence.

Stage 3 of 5 · Testing

Can the bot last three exchanges?

Use your chat window. A strong test has three different messages, not the same keyword repeated. Record what went well and what sounded odd.

Stage 4 of 5 · Break test

Use the right keyword with the wrong meaning

Rule bots can be tricked because they often notice one word but miss the meaning of the whole sentence. Try the break prompts below.

Stage 5 of 5 · Conclusion

Logic is not the same as understanding

Your bot used simple logic: if keyword appears, then reply. Modern AI systems can use context, but they can still make mistakes. The important skill is knowing what kind of system you are using and checking its answers.

Rule-based bot

Looks for keywords. Easy to understand. Breaks when the meaning is more complicated than the rule.

Modern AI assistant

Uses context and patterns from language. More flexible, but still needs checking and clear prompts.
