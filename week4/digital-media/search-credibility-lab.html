🔎 Search & Credibility Lab

Adapted from the digital media worksheet: learn how search engines find websites and how to check whether online information is trustworthy.

Stage 1 of 5 · Search technology

Who crawls the web?

Search engines build an index of pages using programs often called spiders or web crawlers. They follow links from page to page, collecting information so searches can be fast later.

Stage 2 of 5 · Ranking

Why is one result at the top?

A search engine may rank pages by relevance, reliability, freshness, links from trusted sites and how useful the content seems. It should not simply be about which page looks brightest.

Stage 3 of 5 · Better searches

Be more specific than “Romans”

A single broad word often gives too many results. Specific phrases help the search engine understand the exact information you need.

Stage 4 of 5 · Evaluating content

URL detective

A URL can give clues about who made a website. Look for the domain name and endings such as .ac.uk or .org.

www.bbc.co.uk
www.oxford.ac.uk
www.historymuseum.org

Flying penguin claim

A website says a new species of flying penguin has been discovered in Antarctica. What should you do before believing or sharing it?

Helpful checks include: find the original source, compare with trusted news/science sites, check the date, look for evidence, and ask whether the claim sounds too surprising to trust without proof.

Stage 5 of 5 · Finish

Search and credibility summary

Pull everything together. A good digital searcher knows how pages are found, how to search carefully and how to check claims before trusting them.
