Mission briefing

Your job is not to say "plastic bad, paper good". Your job is to think like a designer: packaging has a purpose, but poor packaging creates waste.

Mission 1: What does a wrapper actually do?

Pick the jobs a chocolate wrapper needs to do. The best answer is not "wrap it in nothing". Food packaging has jobs, but it should not be wasteful.

Deep link for older learners: A wrapper can reduce food waste by protecting food, but it can also create material waste. Good design balances both problems instead of pretending only one exists.

Mission 2: Investigate five wrappers

Tap each chocolate bar. Look at the trade-offs: protection, plastic use, recycling, reuse and whether the design is over-packaged.

Mission 5: Test and improve

Real designers test ideas. A wrapper that looks good still has to survive bags, heat, shops, allergies and waste.

Boss level: Is paper always better?

The Plastic Phantom is trying to make the debate too simple. Decide whether each claim is true, false or it depends.

"This trial is rigged! Rigged, I say! ...Fine. Keep your honest labels." · the Plastic Phantom, losing again

16+ challenge link: This is life-cycle thinking. A material can look better at first but still need energy, water, transport, reuse or specialist recycling. The best answer often depends on how the product is used and what happens after.
