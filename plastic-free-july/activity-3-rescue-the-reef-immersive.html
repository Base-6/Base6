Mission 2: Rescue the animals

Tap an animal, then tap the plastic problem that could harm it. Some answers are obvious. Some need a careful think.

Deep dive: why animals get mixed up

Animals do not read labels. They react to shape, movement, smell and where food usually appears. That is why a bag, line or tiny plastic piece can become a real problem.

Mission 3: Tiny plastic, big problem

Build the food chain from smallest to biggest. Then run the chain to see how tiny plastic can move through living things.

Deep dive: why tiny can be tricky

Whole bottles are easier to see and pick up. Microplastics are tiny, spread out, and can be swallowed by small living things.

Key idea: plastic does not need to be large to be a problem. Tiny pieces can still travel.

Mission 4: Rebuild the reef

Make realistic choices. The aim is not to be perfect. The aim is to reduce harm in a way people might actually do.

Deep dive: reduce, reuse, recycle

Reduce means use less in the first place. Reuse means use the same thing again. Recycle helps, but only when the item can really be recycled and ends up in the right place.

Mission 5: The Plastic Phantom's courtroom

The Phantom is trying to confuse the jury. Read each claim and decide whether it is true, too simple, or sneaky wording.

Deep dive: critical thinking

Some claims sound neat but miss important details. A good answer can be: true, false, partly true, or too simple.
